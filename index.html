Chong Han

I am a master’s student in Applied Mathematics and Computational Science at the University of Pennsylvania, with a background in mathematics, learning, and optimal control. I am currently advised by Prof. Nikolai Matni, working on action chunking, imitation learning, and control theory.

Previously, I earned my B.S. in Mathematics of Computation at UCLA. At UCLA, I was advised by Prof. Chen Tang and studied reliable autonomous systems, safety-critical control, and robotics.

Selected Publications

  1. Adding–swapping mappings for k-th power permutations in Sₙ
    Yomi Anifowoshe and Chong Han
    Ars Combinatoria, 167, 213–223, 2026
    paper

    A study of adding–swapping insertion maps for higher-power permutations, using cycle-count signatures and local residue transitions to characterize the structure and limitations of these maps.

  2. Seizure-Semiology-Suite (S³): A Clinically Multimodal Dataset, Benchmark, and Models for Seizure Semiology Understanding
    Lina Zhang, Tonmoy Monsoor, Peizheng Li, Jiarui Cui, Xinyi Peng, Chong Han, et al.
    International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026
    Spotlight
    paper

    A clinically grounded multimodal dataset and benchmark for studying how machine learning models interpret seizure semiology, from visual features to structured reports.

  3. Can Multimodal Large Language Models Understand Pathologic Movements? A Pilot Study on Seizure Semiology
    Lina Zhang, Tonmoy Monsoor, Mehmet Efe Lorasdagi, Prateik Sinha, Chong Han, et al.
    IEEE Engineering in Medicine and Biology Conference (EMBC), 2026
    paper

    An evaluation of multimodal language models on clinical seizure recordings, with feature-specific preprocessing and expert assessment of their explanations.

  4. Crisis-dependent linkages in major exchange rates
    Oyakhilome Ibhagui, James Evans, Tolulope Fadina, Florian Gerth, and Chong Han
    Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions and Money, 109, 102333, 2026
    paper

    An empirical study of how the relationships among major exchange rates vary across crisis and non-crisis periods.
